# Research: Cầu vồng xuất hiện ra sao?

## 1. Main idea (one sentence)
Sunlight entering round raindrops is bent (refracted) by different amounts for each colour, reflects once off the back of the drop and bends again on the way out, so each colour leaves at its own angle (red ~42°, violet ~40° from the point opposite the Sun); every drop sends you just one colour, and all the drops at the right angle together draw a circle of colours centred on your own shadow.

## 2. Process, in order
1. Sunlight looks white but is a mix of all colours (wavelengths). (Met Office)
2. Light enters a raindrop and slows and bends (refraction). Water's refractive index is a little higher for violet than red, so violet bends most and red least. This is dispersion. (Met Office; OpenStax Table 25.2)
3. The light reflects once off the inside back surface of the drop. (Met Office; NWS Flagstaff)
4. It bends again as it leaves the drop, which spreads the colours further. (Met Office)
5. Light comes back concentrated at ~42° from the antisolar line for red and ~40° for violet/blue. (WMO Cloud Atlas; ASU notes; NWS: "The angle created at point D is 42 degrees.")
6. Each drop sends only ONE colour to your eye. Red reaches you from drops ~42° from the antisolar point, violet from drops ~40°, so the colours come from different drops at slightly different heights. Red sits on the outside of the primary bow and violet on the inside. (ASU: "every raindrop 42° of the antisolar line is reflecting red light back to our eyes"; WMO)
7. Geometry: Sun behind you, rain in front. The bow is a circle centred on the antisolar point, the point directly opposite the Sun, which is where the shadow of your head falls. (NWS Flagstaff; atoptics; WMO)
8. Sun altitude: if the Sun is higher than ~42°, the primary bow is entirely below the horizon. The lower the Sun, the higher the bow; at sunrise/sunset you see a full half-circle. (NWS: "The sun needs to be less than 42° above the horizon"; WMO)
9. Full circle: the rainbow is really a full circle. The ground cuts off the lower half, because there are no raindrops below you. From aircraft, helicopters or drones you can sometimes see the whole ring. (Met Office; NASA APOD 2014; USGS photo)
10. Secondary bow: two internal reflections. It is fainter and has its colours reversed (red inside ~51°, violet outside ~54°). (WMO; NWS "around 50°"; Britannica "about 50°"; atoptics)
11. Alexander's dark band: the sky between the two bows is darker, because drops there cannot send light to your eye. Alexander of Aphrodisias described it c. 200 AD. (atoptics; Met Office) The sky inside the primary bow is brighter. (atoptics)
12. Each person sees their own rainbow, made by a different set of drops. Move and your rainbow moves with you. It is not an object at a place, so you can never reach its end. (ASU: "If you are admiring a rainbow with a friend, you are both seeing different rainbows.")

## 4. Misconceptions and nuance
- **"Each drop shows a whole rainbow."** Each drop does spread out all the colours, but in different directions, so only one colour from a given drop reaches your eye. The full rainbow comes from millions of drops at different angles.
- **"A rainbow has exactly 7 colours."** It is a continuous spectrum with far more hues than we can name. Newton popularised seven, adding orange and indigo partly by analogy with the seven musical notes (the music link is standard history but was not checked in this pass). Met Office: "Isaac Newton popularised the idea of seven distinct colours… a rainbow contains a continuous range of hues."
- **"Violet is on the outside."** Wrong for the primary bow: red is outside and violet inside. Only the secondary bow is reversed.
- **"A rainbow is at a fixed place / you can reach its end."** It is a direction (an angle from your shadow), not an object. Walk and it moves with you, and two people see different rainbows.
- **"Rainbows are arches."** They are full circles; the ground hides the lower part.
- **"Rainbows appear at midday."** Usually not: with the Sun above ~42° the primary bow is below the horizon (except from a high viewpoint or in spray below you).
- **"A raindrop works like a prism, so it's just refraction."** The reflection off the back is essential; it is what sends the light back towards you, with the Sun behind you.

## 6. Safe wording for the script
Safe (exactly true):
- "Ánh sáng trắng của Mặt Trời là hỗn hợp của nhiều màu."
- "Vào giọt nước, ánh sáng bị khúc xạ; ánh tím lệch nhiều nhất, ánh đỏ lệch ít nhất."
- "Ánh sáng phản xạ một lần ở mặt sau giọt nước rồi khúc xạ lần nữa khi đi ra."
- "Mỗi giọt chỉ gửi đến mắt bạn một màu; các màu đến từ những giọt khác nhau."
- "Màu đỏ đến từ các giọt lệch khoảng bốn mươi hai độ so với bóng đầu của bạn, màu tím khoảng bốn mươi độ."
- "Đỏ nằm ngoài, tím nằm trong (ở cầu vồng chính)."
- "Mặt Trời phải ở sau lưng bạn và thấp hơn khoảng bốn mươi hai độ."
- "Cầu vồng thật ra là một vòng tròn; mặt đất che mất nửa dưới. Từ trên trực thăng hay máy bay đôi khi thấy được cả vòng."
- "Mỗi người nhìn thấy một cầu vồng của riêng mình."
- "Cầu vồng phụ mờ hơn, nằm ngoài, màu đảo ngược, do ánh sáng phản xạ hai lần."

Avoid:
- "cầu vồng có đúng bảy màu" -> say "ta thường kể bảy màu, nhưng thật ra màu chuyển liên tục".
- "mỗi giọt nước tạo ra một cầu vồng" / "giọt nước tách màu rồi cho ta thấy cả dải".
- "cầu vồng ở chỗ kia" / "đi tới chân cầu vồng".
- "giọt nước giống hệt lăng kính" (OK: "giống một lăng kính nhỏ, cộng thêm một lần phản xạ").
- Exact decimals as if universal (40,5°; 1,343); "lần đầu tiên / duy nhất / ai đó phát hiện ra" unless sourced (Descartes and Newton are history; check before naming).
- "hiếm khi thấy cầu vồng thứ cấp": it is fairly common, just faint (OpenStax calls it "rare"; sources disagree, so avoid the claim).
