# Brief — "Vì sao ta ngáp — và vì sao ngáp lây?" (Vạn vật vận hành như thế nào, 20/11/2026)

**Idea (one line):** yawning is not about oxygen (pure oxygen does not stop it — Provine 1987); the best-supported hypothesis is that a yawn cools the brain: the jaw stretches, more blood flows through the head, a deep breath of cooler air takes heat from that blood, cooler blood reaches the brain. And it is contagious: seeing, hearing, even reading about a yawn makes about half to two-thirds of people yawn too.
**Device:** the hero is THE BRAIN (`heroBrain` in props.js: a pink paper cutout with folds and a face). It floats above Minh's head in the outside scenes; inside the cut-away profile head it sits in the skull. Its temperature is shown by colour (orange flush + 3 orange heat wisps = warm; plain pink = cool) — use `heroWarmAt(TT)` for the hero's `warm` value everywhere. **The end is the start, changed:** the same desk as the hook, but now Minh AND Lan yawn, the brain is cool and fans itself (`fan: 1`), and a tag points at the viewer ("bạn?").

**Format:** F5 fixed-hero journey, morph-joined on the hero; one hard cut (SLAM 42.5 s) into "payoff" inside the pause after "lay". **Style:** cut-paper (torn shapes, soft shadows, Pangolin text) — KHUNG HÌNH GỌN: flat fills, light grain (overridden in props.js), NO dotted patterns (`pat.dots` banned), ≤ 1 small-particle effect per scene. 9:16, 1080×1920, 24 fps, 120 BPM grid, 60.75 s, Vietnamese voice-over (Tuan Khang, eleven_v3).

**Colour = one meaning:** ORANGE `COL.heat*` = heat / warm · BLUE `COL.cool*` = cool air / cooling / winter · DEEP RED `COL.blood*` = blood · PINK `COL.brain*` = the brain (hero) only — never use pink or orange for anything else (shirts: blue Minh, mustard Lan, purple mom, green kid, charcoal stranger).

## Claims + sources (research.md)
- Provine et al. 1987: breathing 100% O2 / CO2 mixes / exercise changed breathing but not yawning → "not about oxygen".
- Brain cooling = the best-supported HYPOTHESIS (Gallup & Gallup 2007; Shoup-Knox 2010; Gallup & Eldakar 2011) — script says "giả thuyết mạnh nhất".
- Arizona, Gallup & Eldakar 2011: contagious yawning 45% in winter (22 °C) vs 24% in summer (37 °C).
- Contagious yawning ~41–68% across studies → "khoảng một nửa đến hai phần ba".
- Norscia & Palagi 2011: kin > friends > acquaintances > strangers. Helt 2010: most children don't catch yawns before ~4.
