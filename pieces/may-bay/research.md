# Research: Máy bay nặng hàng trăm tấn sao vẫn bay được?
Checked 2026-11-18. ✔ = verified in a fetched source; ~ = approximate or secondary.

## 1. Core idea (one sentence)
The wing, tilted slightly upward (angle of attack) and curved (camber), turns a huge mass of air downward every second. The air pushes back up on the wing, which shows up as lower pressure above the wing and higher pressure below. That push is the lift. In level flight lift ≈ weight. The engines supply only thrust (forward speed), not the lift. Lift grows with the square of airspeed, so a plane needs a long runway to build enough speed.

- NASA lift equation: L = Cl · (ρ·V²/2) · A. Lift depends on "the square of the velocity". ✔
  https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/lift-equation/
- NASA: "The integrated velocity variation around the object produces a net turning of the gas flow" → Newton's 3rd law gives the force. "So both 'Bernoulli' and 'Newton' are correct." Both the upper and lower surfaces turn the flow. ✔
  https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/bernoulli-and-newton
- Anderson & Eberhardt (Fermilab / Univ. of Washington): "The lift of a wing is equal to the change in momentum of the air it diverts down". Angle of attack "is the primary parameter in determining lift". ✔
  https://aviation-history.com/theory/lift.htm
- FAA PHAK Ch.5 Aerodynamics of Flight: "In level flight, lift opposes the downward force of weight." ✔ (search snippet)
  https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/07_phak_ch5_0.pdf

## 2. Process, in order
1. The engines push the plane forward along the runway (thrust). Speed builds up.
2. Flaps (trailing edge) and slats (leading edge) are already set to a moderate takeoff position. They add wing area and camber, giving more lift at low speed. ✔ NASA: https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/flaps-and-slats
3. As speed rises, lift rises with V². Even so, at first lift is still less than the weight.
4. At rotation speed (VR) the pilot raises the nose, which increases the angle of attack, so the wing turns more air downward.
5. Lift > weight → the wheels leave the ground → climb.
6. Later the flaps and slats retract. At cruise the high speed gives enough lift at a small angle of attack, and lift ≈ weight.
(Optional point: lift falls off when the angle of attack exceeds about 15° (stall), so the pilot cannot just tilt up more and more. ✔ Anderson & Eberhardt)

## 4. Misconceptions to avoid
- **Equal transit time** ("the air over the top must travel farther to meet the air below at the same time"): WRONG. NASA: "Two molecules near each other at the leading edge will not end up next to each other at the trailing edge". The air over the top actually arrives FIRST. The theory also cannot explain symmetric wings, paper planes or upside-down flight.
  https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/VirtualAero/BottleRocket/airplane/wrong1.html
- **"The engines lift the plane up"**: wrong. The engines push the plane forward (thrust). The wings make the lift.
- **"Only Bernoulli is right" or "only Newton is right"**: wrong. They are two ways of describing the same flow (NASA bernoulli-and-newton). The safe wording is "cánh hất không khí xuống, không khí đẩy cánh lên; áp suất trên cánh thấp hơn dưới cánh" (the wing throws air down and the air pushes the wing up; pressure above the wing is lower than below). Both statements are true together.
- **"Air hits the underside of the wing like a skipping stone"**: incomplete. NASA says both surfaces turn the flow, and the upper surface matters a lot.

## Safest script claims
1. Wings make lift by turning air downward; the air pushes the wing up (pressure below > pressure above).
2. Engines give forward speed, not lift.
3. Double the speed → four times the lift, which is why planes need a long runway.
4. At takeoff the pilot raises the nose (bigger angle of attack) and the cánh tà are extended to get more lift at low speed.
5. An A380 can take off at up to 575 tonnes. Airliners leave the ground at around 250–290 km/h.
