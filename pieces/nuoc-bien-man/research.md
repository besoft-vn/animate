# Research: Vì sao nước biển mặn?

## 1. Main idea (one sentence)
Rain slightly acidified by CO2 slowly dissolves rock on land, rivers carry the dissolved ions to the sea, evaporation takes away only the water, and over millions of years the ions (plus chloride from volcanic gases and minerals from seafloor vents) have built up to a roughly steady ~3.5% salt, kept there by an input/removal balance.

## 2. Process, in order
1. Rain absorbs CO2 from the air -> weak carbonic acid -> rain is slightly acidic. (USGS)
2. Acidic rain weathers (phong hóa) rock on land, releasing dissolved ions. Main *river* ions are bicarbonate, calcium, silica; sodium and chloride are present only in small amounts. (Penn State EARTH 111; Oberlin table)
3. Rivers carry the dissolved ions to the sea. (USGS, NOAA)
4. Evaporation removes nearly pure water; the salts stay behind. Rain/rivers then bring in fresh water again, and the cycle repeats.
5. Ions with long residence times (sodium, chloride) build up; ions that organisms or chemistry remove quickly (calcium, bicarbonate, silica) do not.
6. Extra sources: hydrothermal vents (miệng phun thủy nhiệt) and submarine volcanoes add dissolved minerals. Vent fluids pick up metals such as iron, zinc and copper and lose magnesium and sulfate. (NOAA; USGS says the whole ocean volume circulates through vents every ~10–20 million years.)
7. Chloride nuance: the main *anions* of seawater (chloride above all) are mostly "excess volatiles" from volcanic degassing (e.g., HCl gas), not from rock weathering. Sodium comes mainly from weathering, delivered by rivers. (W. M. White, *Geochemistry*, ch. 15)
8. Removal (sinks): organisms take up calcium and bicarbonate (carbonate shells) and silica; ions are buried in sediments, exchanged with seafloor rock and vents, and locked up in salt deposits (evaporites). (NOAA "organisms remove many ions"; Britannica on salt locked in seafloor deposits)
9. Steady state: input is roughly balanced by removal. Salinity is about constant on human timescales and has likely been near steady state for at least 100 million years. (Britannica "The modern oceans"; White ch. 15; Open University)

## 4. Misconceptions and nuance
- **"Rivers are fresh, so where does the salt come from?"** Rivers do carry dissolved salts, just very little of them (~0.12 g/L vs ~35 g/L, about 300x less), too little to taste. Safe: "Nước sông cũng có muối hòa tan, chỉ rất ít nên ta không thấy mặn."
- **"Rivers are just weaker seawater."** Wrong composition: river ions are mostly bicarbonate and calcium, while seawater is mostly chloride and sodium. Seawater is not concentrated river water. Calcium and bicarbonate are removed quickly (shells, sediments); sodium and chloride stay for a very long time and build up.
- **"All sea salt comes from rocks."** Sodium (and much of Mg, K, Ca) comes from weathered rock. Chloride, the largest ion, comes mainly from volcanic gases released over Earth's history. Vents and seafloor reactions also change composition.
- **"The sea gets saltier every year."** No. Input is roughly balanced by removal (steady state). The ocean has not been piling up salt endlessly (Joly's "age from salt" estimate failed for this reason). Local salinity does vary with evaporation, rain, river input and ice.
- **"Evaporation makes salt."** Evaporation only concentrates salt by removing water; it does not create salt.
- **"Salinity is the same everywhere."** It varies regionally: higher in mid-latitude subtropics, lower near the equator, the poles and river mouths (NOAA). The *ratio* of ions is nearly constant, though.

## 6. "Flip" opening fact
Requested phrasing: "Muối biển phần lớn đến từ đá trên đất liền, do nước mưa mang đi." This is half-true. It holds for sodium and the cations (USGS: "Salt in the ocean comes from rocks on land"), but chloride, which is 55% of sea salt by mass, comes mainly from volcanic gases. Avoid an unqualified "phần lớn".

Safe options (best first):
1. "Nước sông mà ta uống được cũng mang muối ra biển, chỉ là quá ít để nếm thấy." (rivers ~300x less salty; accurate and counter-intuitive)
2. "Một phần muối biển bắt nguồn từ đá trên đất liền, bị nước mưa hòa tan từng chút một suốt hàng triệu năm."
3. "Nếu rải hết muối trong đại dương lên đất liền, ta sẽ có một lớp dày hơn một trăm sáu mươi mét." (USGS)
4. If sodium/chloride is mentioned: "Natri trong muối biển chủ yếu đến từ đá bị phong hóa, còn clorua phần lớn đến từ khí núi lửa phun ra trong lịch sử Trái Đất."

Avoid: "biển ngày càng mặn hơn", "muối biển chỉ đến từ sông", "lần đầu tiên/duy nhất", and exact single residence-time figures.
